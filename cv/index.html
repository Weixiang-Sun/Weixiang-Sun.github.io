Research Summary

  • I aim to build a science of AI–AI interaction. HCI and HAI study how people interact with computers and AI; as AI agents increasingly collaborate with, evaluate, and stand in for one another, I study them the way sociology studies people, from the individual agent to micro-level interaction to macro-level societies. A recurring lesson is that what holds for one agent does not hold for many: collaboration between agents is not free and does not always pay off, interaction can erode the diversity that makes a group valuable, and agents standing in for people flatten the variety of real populations. I want to understand these dynamics well enough to make systems of many AI agents reliable and safe.
    • Agent. How one agent plans, acts, and learns: computer-use agents in production (Li Auto), long-horizon planning, self-evolving agents, and agents that serve people in social services.
    • Micro. How agents shape each other: when collaboration pays off, how interaction shapes collective output, and multi-agent systems for creative and scientific work; as a human reference, how manipulation unfolds in conversation.
    • Macro. How agent populations behave and stand in for people: how faithfully LLMs simulate individuals and social roles, and simulating how policies play out across populations.
